Contact-Triggered Sequential Grasping With an HSA-Driven Underactuated Gripper for Delicate Produce

Xiaoqian Zhang, Mario Baggetta, Cristina Piazza, and Giovanni Berselli
IEEE/ASME Transactions on Mechatronics, vol. 31, no. 4, pp. 4828-4836, 2026.
DOI: 10.1109/TMECH.2026.3696423

Who may find this useful

Researchers developing underactuated hands, adaptive end-effectors, and robotic handling of delicate produce.

What the mechanism contributes

  • Passive target tracking after early contact

  • A single actuator drives both mechanical subsystems

Evidence & scope

Finite-element analysis and benchtop experiments evaluate offsets, object sizes, pull-out behavior, pinch-only operation, and side-entry grasping. These are prototype-level results; field harvesting and biological bruise reduction are not established by this demonstration.

Abstract

A main challenge in agri-tech lies in robotic handling and harvesting of delicate produce. Traditional end-effectors struggle to balance grip stability, adaptability to irregular items, and bruise mitigation. In this work, a soft gripper based on handed shearing auxetic (HSA) structures is presented. The gripper adopts a dual-module architecture composed of a target tracking system (TTS) and a pinch and grasp system (PGS). A passive tendon-driven differential shifts the grasping center after early contact, compensating for target misalignment without active sensors. The PGS employs HSA units to achieve transition from parallel pinching to bending-based enclosure through contact-induced torque redirection. Both modules share a single actuator and preserve a minimal transmission architecture. Finite element simulations and response surface exploration identify key stiffness and geometric parameters, guiding the rigid-body synthesis of the TTS and actuator selection. Experiments on objects with varying offsets, diameters, and pull-out conditions, including pinch-only and side-entry tests, assess the ability to self-center, pinch, and enclose without additional control layers. The results indicate that HSA actuation combined with passive differentials can provide a compact mechanism-level solution for produce-oriented grasping under representative benchtop conditions.
